parser. The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first (dayFirst in causes.ts asks the platform, locale by locale), so 07/04 typed in the app or sent by email means 7 April to them and 4 July to the parser. Reaches every tenant, through the app, email and the booking-v2 form.

hint. Three tenants run booking-v3. Its form prints MM/DD/YYYY under the date field, so their people type the month first as told, and the form then reads it the tenant’s way, day first. Same symptom, a flipped month, but a different bug in a different place. Only reports that came in through the form from a booking-v3 tenant are this problem.

one-day-move. Five reports are not a month flip at all: the date moved by one day, an invite came in before its booking, or two time zones got two different days. The desk read them as a one-day move and wrote it in its notes; they stay counted under parser, where the desk filed them, and get a ticket of their own on top.

Why the merge hid this

  • Reports were merged by subject line (decision 0001), and all twelve subjects describe the symptom, not the cause. The same subject covers all three problems.
  • Intake tagged each report from the reporter’s browser locale, not from the tenant. The table above counts how many tags point at the wrong cause. The tag is a guess; tenant and channel are the fact.
  • The export overlaps between pages, so 15 reports appear twice. uniqueById already handles it; the numbers here are deduplicated the same way.

What to do with #4471

  2. Open the hint and one-day-move tickets as their own work; they are filed and linked above.
